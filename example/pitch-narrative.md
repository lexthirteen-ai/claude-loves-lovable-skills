# Pitch narrative: Lather & Lane

**Audience:** pre-seed angel investors with small-business software experience
**The ask:** $150K to reach 300 paying groomers in 12 months
**One-line pitch:** Booking and driving-order day plans for solo mobile groomers, because a groomer's real cost is the drive between baths.

## Headlines (skim test)
1. Mobile groomers lose two hours a day driving
2. The schedule is the route
3. Clients book. The day plans itself.
4. Three taps from booking to a planned day
5. Every solo mobile groomer is a $29-a-month customer
6. $29 a month, paid by the groomer
7. Groomers are asking for this before it exists
8. Their regulars live here
9. Built by a former mobile groomer
10. $150K to reach 300 paying groomers

## Slides

### 1. Problem
- **Claim:** Solo mobile groomers lose hours every day to badly ordered routes.
- **Proof:** In 12 interviews, groomers estimated 1.5 to 2.5 hours of driving per day, and most booked by text in whatever order clients replied.
- **Headline:** Mobile groomers lose two hours a day driving
- **Support:** Clients book by text, in random order. The groomer zig-zags across town to keep up.
- **Speaker notes:** Every groomer we talked to books the same way: texts, a paper calendar, and a lot of apologizing. Nobody plans the route, because the bookings arrive in the order clients happen to reply. That drive is unpaid time, and it is the difference between five dogs a day and seven.

### 2. Insight
- **Claim:** For a mobile groomer, booking and routing are one problem, not two.
- **Proof:** Groomers in interviews already turn down bookings that are "too far that day". They are routing in their heads.
- **Headline:** The schedule is the route
- **Support:** Booking tools ignore the drive. Route apps ignore the bookings. Groomers need both at once.
- **Speaker notes:** Existing booking tools were built for salons, where the client comes to you. Mobile groomers go to the client, so when a booking lands decides how much the day costs. Offer clients only the slots that fit the route, and the day plans itself.

### 3. Product
- **Claim:** Clients only see slots that fit the groomer's route.
- **Proof:** The prototype offers slots by neighborhood and day. Five of five test groomers understood it without explanation.
- **Headline:** Clients book. The day plans itself.
- **Support:** A booking link for clients, and a driving-order day plan for the groomer.
- **Speaker notes:** The groomer shares one link. Clients pick from times that make sense for their address. The groomer opens the app in the morning and sees the day in driving order, with addresses and dog notes.

### 4. How it works

- **Support:** Set your service area. Share your link. Open today's route.
- **Speaker notes:** We designed for a groomer sitting in the van between appointments. Every screen works one-handed on a phone.

### 5. Market
- **Claim:** There are enough solo mobile groomers to build a real business.
- **Proof:** [NEEDS PROOF: count of US mobile grooming businesses from an industry association or business registries]
- **Headline:** Every solo mobile groomer is a $29-a-month customer
- **Support:** [NEEDS PROOF: US count] solo groomers × $29 a month, before multi-van teams.
- **Speaker notes:** We are not sizing all pet care. We count the people who would actually pay: solo operators with a van. Multi-van teams are the expansion.

### 6. Business model
- **Claim:** Groomers pay a flat monthly fee that one extra dog a month covers.
- **Proof:** An average mobile groom costs more than $29. One extra dog pays for the month.
- **Headline:** $29 a month, paid by the groomer
- **Support:** No per-booking fees. Clients book free.
- **Speaker notes:** Groomers hate per-booking fees because they feel like a tax on their regulars. A flat fee is easy to say yes to.
